Catalysis & Activation Energy

A catalyst increases the rate of reaction by providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy (Ea). It does not alter state functions such as enthalpy change (ΔH).

Ea_catalyzed < Ea_uncatalyzed  |  ΔH_catalyzed = ΔH_uncatalyzed
Second-Order Kinetics

The rate of a second-order reaction is proportional to the square of reactant concentration. Slope of 1/[A] versus time is equal to the rate constant (k).

Rate = k[A]²  |  Units of k: L/(mol·s)  |  1/[A] - 1/[A]₀ = kt
Arrhenius Model

The temperature dependence of rate constants is exponential. A 10°C rise in temperature typically doubles the rate constant (k) by increasing the fraction of collision molecules with sufficient energy, while the activation energy itself remains unchanged.

k = A · e^(-Ea/RT)  |  ln(k₂/k₁) = (Ea/R) · (1/T₁ - 1/T₂)
